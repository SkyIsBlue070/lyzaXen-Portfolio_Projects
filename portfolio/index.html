How we’ll work

Four steps.
No surprises.

  1. 01

    Clarify the brief

    You share the goal, the audience, examples you like and any content or footage you already have. I ask questions until the brief is clear.

  2. 02

    Agree the scope

    I send a written scope: deliverables, what’s included, revision rounds, timeline and price. Work starts once you approve it.

  3. 03

    Build and review

    You see progress at agreed checkpoints — a preview link for web work, draft exports for edits — and reply with one consolidated list of changes per round.

  4. 04

    Hand over

    You receive the final files or the deployed site, the source code, and short notes on how to update or reuse what we made.

Feedback and revisions

Revision rounds are part of the agreed scope. Changes within the brief are covered. New features or a change of direction aren’t refused — they’re scoped and quoted separately, and I don’t start them until you approve.

Practical questions

Good to know
before we start.

What can you take on?

Business websites, landing pages, small web apps and features for existing sites, plus short-form video editing. If a project needs something outside that — native mobile apps at scale, complex backends, long-form video — I’ll tell you honestly during the brief rather than stretch.

How long does a project take?

It depends on scope, how ready your content is and how quickly feedback comes back. A landing page or a single short is usually counted in days; a multi-page site or a web app in weeks. You get a dated timeline in the written scope before anything starts.

How do revisions work?

The scope lists how many revision rounds are included. One round is one consolidated list of changes. Changes within the brief are covered; new features or a change of direction are quoted separately first.

What do I need to provide?

For websites: your text, logo, images and any accounts (domain, hosting) — or a note on what you need help with. For editing: the raw footage, any brand fonts or colours, and music you’re licensed to use. Examples you like help a lot.

Do you use AI?

Yes, as a tool. I use AI coding assistants to explore approaches, draft routine code and check my work faster. I review and test everything before it reaches you, and I’m responsible for the result. If your project has rules about AI tools or confidential material, tell me and we’ll agree them in the scope.

How will we communicate?

Mostly by email or the messaging channel we agree, with calls scheduled when a conversation is faster. I’m on IST (UTC+5:30) and will confirm reply times and check-in points in the scope.

What happens at handover?

Websites and apps: the deployed site or deployment instructions, the full source code and short update notes. Edits: the final exports in the agreed formats. You own the deliverables once the project is paid for.

Are the projects here client work?

No — and they’re labelled accordingly. Afterhours and SignalDesk are concept projects with fictional content, Zoi is a health app I independently rebuilt and improved from an existing team project, and the rest are personal projects. The screenshots are of the actual builds; the Zoi card is a title card, not a screenshot.
